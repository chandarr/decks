# 01 · Pressure test & assumptions (the gate — agreed)

## Weak joints and their defensible reframes

1. **Unverified numbers are a live hazard in a document whose purpose is to prove rigor.** KUKA India FY financials (₹200–300cr band, ~57% growth, ~46% PAT decline), payload figures (1,300 vs 1,500 kg), iiQKA.OS specifics from the Google thread (IEC 62443-4-2 "first-in-class", launch dates), the ABB Robotics India headcount (350–500, which is Google's *derived* estimate from a revenue-proportion calculation), and the India-specific FANUC claims (Open Platform launch, InBolt/Stream Motion deployments) sourced to Instagram and LinkedIn posts.
   → Every on-screen figure lives in `data.js`, carries an evidence tier, and is swappable without touching layout. Derived estimates state their method — which reads as *more* rigorous, not less. Instagram-sourced competitive claims are either verified against FANUC India's own channels or restated at the level that is solid ("FANUC has opened its architecture to ROS 2/Python and is pushing physical-AI capability toward emerging-market SMEs").

2. **The team critique must be structural, never personal.** Naming pedigree, universities, or "not an A-team" reads as someone who leads by disparaging inherited teams — a quiet disqualifier for a leadership candidate.
   → "The entity was built correctly for a trader-support mandate; the mandate no longer matches the market." Reinforced by the revenue-per-head observation: KUKA India is *more* revenue-efficient per head than ABB Robotics India, which is exactly what a well-run trading operation looks like. Generous to the people, damning to the design, impossible to argue with.

3. **The Wipro PARI departure is unverified — excluded.** Could not be corroborated in any public source; the long-serving former MD traces to his own firm, and Wipro PARI's leadership roster does not include him.
   → Use only the verified instability signal. Do not reintroduce.

4. **The Midea governance question is excluded from the written deck.** A pointed "will Midea share or absorb the benefits" challenge, in writing, pre-offer, reads as presumptuous and creates a problem for the CEO rather than solving one.
   → Midea appears as **opportunity** — Chinese manufacturing strength as one of the four corners — plus one soft line on IP alignment. The sharp governance question is held for the live room, where the candidate can read the situation and let the CEO open the door.

5. **Physical-AI maturity is the timing thesis, not a hedge.** The honest state of the art is that physical AI is spectacular in demos and pilots, and the generalization-to-reliability gap is the industry's central unsolved problem. Industrial customers buy 99.x% uptime, not 90%.
   → State it plainly: physical AI sits roughly where language models sat three years ago, and the winners were positioned *before* capability arrived. Position, don't promise. The readiness gap is precisely *why to move now*. Pair with the honest de-risking path: start human-in-the-loop and semi-structured, prove the reliability curve on real floors, let the flywheel walk autonomy up.

6. **RaaS cuts both ways and the deck must not pretend otherwise.** RaaS is what makes the flywheel spin (owning the fleet turns every deployment into an instrumented data node, and aligns incentives on uptime) — but it also means KUKA owns the reliability risk and eats the cost of every failure. The strength and the risk are the same mechanism.
   → Acknowledge on the flywheel screen rather than letting the advisor find it.

7. **Scope: a synopsis that fires every shell leaves nothing to earn the meeting.**
   → Land the *shape* of the opportunity and the *shape* of the ask. Hold budget, milestones, and team structure for the meeting itself. Restraint reads as seniority.

8. **The candidate must not look like he is skipping the job he is being screened for.** Over-indexing on global paradigm vision invites "will he actually run the India P&L?"
   → The spine resolves this: India is not the job, India is the proving ground for a global thesis, and he is asking to run the proof. Grounded and expansive at once. The global trajectory is framed as *earned on results*, never requested up front.

## Evidence tiers (carried into every screen)

- **Confirmed:** IFR India 9,100 units 2024 (+7%, 6th globally); ABB→SoftBank $5.375B; ABB India robotics transfer to a standalone entity; FANUC–NVIDIA and FANUC–Google/Intrinsic partnerships; KUKA India as a trade/service-classified entity with ~76–100 staff.
- **Estimate:** market CAGRs and TAM projections; cost-arbitrage ratios; robotics-VC totals (sources differ by 2–3x); ABB Robotics India headcount; KUKA's India unit share.
- **Frontier / aspirational:** the flywheel; the net-new unaddressed market; global replication to Vietnam/Indonesia/Mexico/LATAM.

Rendered with distinct chip treatment throughout, so the deck marks its own uncertainty. That self-marking is itself part of the credibility argument.
