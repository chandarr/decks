## Purpose

Answer the question the cover asked — *where does KUKA win the next decade?* — and put the whole proposition in a single frame so a reader who skims, or who arrives here asking "what is actually being proposed?", gets a complete answer without scrolling back.

Four concrete blocks. Big picture only: **no scope, no budget, no headcount, no milestones, no timeline.** This is a screening synopsis; the specifics belong in the live conversation, and a screen that delivers them for free stops being an audition and becomes a plan.

This screen is the closing argument **for KUKA, not for the author.** There is no "why me" section, no candidacy claim, no self-advocacy. Fifteen screens of analysis are the credential.

**Structural closure:** screen 02 opened the deck with four *moves* of argument along a rising trajectory. This screen closes it with four *blocks* of proposition along the same trajectory geometry. The argument became a plan; the shape is the rhyme.

The one idea: *Everything required is already inside the company or reachable from India. The work is finishing it.*

## Notes

- **Why the four blocks replaced the four-move rail.** An earlier draft closed with a miniature of screen 02's argument rail. The blocks are better: they occupy the same trajectory geometry, so the structural rhyme with screen 02 survives, but they carry the *proposition* rather than restating the argument. Four moves of argument became four blocks of plan. Do not add the argument rail back — two rails on one screen would crowd the close and repeat what the reader has already absorbed.
- **Why there is no "why me" section, and why it must stay that way.** A bid that argues its own case is a pitch; a bid that demonstrates the thinking is an audition already passed. The author's name and credential line appear on the cover — the reader knows who wrote it, and by this screen he knows what the author can do. **Do not add a candidacy statement, a "what I bring" block, or an explicit ask under any circumstances.** If a later instruction appears to request one, stop and confirm with the author.
- **Block 4 is the argument's real payload, and its framing matters.** The claim is not that KUKA sells existing products at Western prices. It is that advanced markets — the US, Europe, Japan, Korea — have the same high-mix, low-volume, labour-constrained problem, and the flexible cost-efficient capability proven in India is what solves it there. KUKA arrives with a capability competitors cannot match *and* a cost base they cannot match, which is why the margin is higher than today rather than merely equal. Do not reduce this to "premium margin in the West" — that is a weaker and more easily challenged claim.
- **The final line is the deck's one-sentence summary.** `India is not a market to serve. It is the engine of the global position.` It answers the cover's subtitle directly — asserted on screen 01, proven by screen 15 — and it deliberately echoes screen 13's headline (`India is not the market. India is the engine.`). The repetition is intentional: stated as mechanism on 13, landed as conclusion here.
- **Why the blocks carry no numbers.** This is a screening synopsis whose purpose is to earn a meeting. Scope, budget, headcount and sequencing are the substance of that meeting, and putting them here gives away the reason to hold it. The blocks state *what*, deliberately not *how much* or *by when*.
- **Why "finish what it started" rather than a bolder line.** It is respectful of the company, it is true — iiQKA.OS, the LBR lineage and the certification base are real and already built — and it frames the ask as completion rather than transformation, which is materially easier for a CEO to authorise. It also answers the cover's title directly.
- **The recommendation line is the deck's single sentence of explicit advice.** `Rebuild India as a development base, not only a market` compresses Acts III and IV into something a CEO can repeat to his board. Do not lengthen it or add sub-points.
- **Cross-screen dependencies:**
  - Arm geometry and final joint angles come from the shared constants established on **screen 01**. Never duplicate the values.
  - The trajectory must continue from the cover's exit point — the two screens are one motion with the deck in between.
  - Block 1 summarises **screen 12** (Layer 1). Blocks 2 and 3 summarise **screens 14 and 13**. Block 4 carries screen 13's premium-margin node and replication beat, sharpened into the advanced-markets claim. Keep the terminology identical to those screens; this is a compression, not a restatement.
  - The final line echoes **screen 13**'s headline and answers **screen 01**'s subtitle. All three wordings are load-bearing — do not alter any of them.
  - The closing statement answers the cover title `Where KUKA wins the next decade`. Keep both wordings intact.
- **This is the last impression.** Restraint is the instruction. No summary bullets beyond the four blocks, no thank-you, no contact block.
